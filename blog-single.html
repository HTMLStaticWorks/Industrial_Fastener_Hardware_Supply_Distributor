Preventing Hydrogen Embrittlement In High-Tensile Fasteners

Dr. Linda Chen, PhD September 28, 2026 8 min read

Hydrogen Embrittlement (HE) represents one of the most hazardous failure modes in structural and mechanical engineering. Unlike ductile yield, which provides visual necking and warning signs, hydrogen-induced fractures are sudden, catastrophic, and often occur hours or days after tightening under static load.

The Triad of Embrittlement Failure

For hydrogen embrittlement to transpire in an industrial fastener, three independent criteria must converge simultaneously:

  • High-Strength Susceptible Metallurgy: Steels with a core or surface hardness exceeding 320 HV / 32 HRC (commonly seen in SAE Grade 8, ASTM A490, and ISO Class 10.9 and 12.9 socket head cap screws).
  • Atomic Hydrogen Absorption: Hydrogen atoms generated during acid pickling, electroplating (zinc, cadmium), or in-service galvanic corrosion diffuse into interstitial crystal lattice defects.
  • Sustained Tensile Stress: High elastic preload or clamping force that causes trapped hydrogen atoms to migrate to regions of triaxial stress concentration, such as thread roots and the head-to-shank fillet.

ASTM F1940 Process Control Testing

At IndustrialPro Fasteners, we employ the ASTM F1940 incremental step loading technique. Test specimens are loaded to 75% of notched tensile strength while measuring potential drop. If atomic hydrogen is present, localized micro-cracking is detected electronically within minutes, stopping questionable lots before shipment to customer job sites.

"Never compromise on post-plate baking cycles for fasteners above 39 HRC. The cost of thermal dehydrogenation is pennies per hundred pieces; the cost of a sheared crane bracket or structural failure is catastrophic."

— Dr. Linda Chen, PhD, VP Metallurgy at IndustrialPro Fasteners

Recommended Engineering Countermeasures

For designers specifying fasteners in critical fatigue joints, consider:

  1. Specify non-electrolytic coatings such as mechanically deposited zinc or Magni / Geomet dip-spin coatings which produce zero hydrogen evolution during deposition.
  2. Mandate ASTM B850 baking certificates with lot numbers, oven charts, and time-stamped entry logs.
  3. In corrosive outdoor marine environments, transition from high-strength carbon alloys to A4-80 or 316 austenitic stainless steels that are immune to hydrogen embrittlement.
